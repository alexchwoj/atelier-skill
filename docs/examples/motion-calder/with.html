The Net Comes Up

Small boats, hand-hauled nets and the long quiet between casting and counting.

Nobody on these boats talks while the net is coming up. The work is a rhythm of pull, gather and brace, and the photographs stay inside it, close enough to hear the rope.

The story follows the net from the first haul to the knots mended on the sand. The catch is never the subject; the people holding the line are.

When the River Stayed

Orchards, paddies and fields that kept the water for weeks after the flood.

The flood lasted days. The water stayed for weeks. This story is about the second part, when the news has moved on and the fields are still lakes.

Farmers feed animals knee-deep, race bulls through the mud and wait for a dry edge to start again.

By Hand

Harvests that still run on fingers, baskets and backs.

Some crops refuse machines. Saffron is picked flower by flower, olives are sorted from leaves by hand, wheat is still carried in sheaves where the fields are small.

The pictures stay at hand height, where the work is decided.

Stall Light

Market halls where one shaft of sun decides who gets seen.

A market is a room built out of light. Sellers arrange their goods where the sun or the bulb will find them, and lean into it themselves.

These frames were made by waiting for the light to reach a face.

Heat Trades

Forges, tanneries, glass and steel: work done close to fire and noise.

These are jobs you can hear before you see them. Hammering, the roar of a furnace, the hiss of a pressure washer on a hull.

The story moves from the oldest trade in it to the newest, and keeps the same distance from each worker.

Last Train Home

Platforms, rain and the people who travel after the city closes.

After midnight the city belongs to the people who keep it running. Cleaners, rail crews and night-shift workers share the last services and the wet streets between them.

The story is shot the way the night looks: mostly grey, with one colour when a train arrives.
